# Social and competitive design

State the relationship the system should create: cooperation, recognition,
teaching, rivalry, belonging, or shared story. Define the action, reward,
power asymmetry, abuse path, and newcomer path.

| System | Questions | Guardrails |
|---|---|---|
| Multiplayer feature | Who depends on whom, and when can coordination fail? | Avoid hostage states and hidden advantage. |
| Session chapters | What social beat opens, turns, resolves, and invites return? | Give every player a legible contribution. |
| Leaderboard | What metric, cohort, period, reset, and reward are fair? | Prefer peers, friends, personal bests, or seasons to global dominance. |
| Social satisfaction | Does it support autonomy, competence, relatedness, or status? | Status must not require humiliation or exclusion. |

Build leaderboards from purpose before presentation. Specify eligibility,
anti-cheat, tie handling, visibility, reward limits, seasonal migration, and a
non-ranked alternative. Audit for toxic incentives, collaboration sabotage,
pay advantage, harassment, and new-player futility. Measure voluntary
participation and sentiment beside rank engagement.
