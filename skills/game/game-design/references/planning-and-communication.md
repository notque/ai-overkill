# Prototyping, planning, and communication

Prototype to reduce uncertainty, not to accumulate a tiny final game.

| Unknown | Cheapest useful test |
|---|---|
| Is the core action satisfying? | Greybox or paper interaction |
| Do players understand the goal? | Clickable flow or moderated task |
| Does the system support depth? | Small rules simulation |
| Is the fantasy compelling? | Pitch, animatic, or vertical-slice moment |
| Can the team deliver it? | Technical spike with exit criteria |

End each prototype with continue, revise, defer, or stop. Before prioritizing,
generate at least three alternatives, including a cut. Compare player value,
pillar fit, evidence confidence, effort, dependencies, and reversibility.
Estimate scope as ranges with assumptions across design, art, engineering,
audio, QA, production, and operations.

For emotional direction, name player state, sensory cues, interaction, contrast,
and rejection criteria. Moodboards are references, not specifications; use only
owned or licensed production assets. Critique concrete moments: inferred intent,
observation, tension, impact, and the smallest testable option.

Estimate as a range with assumptions, confidence, dependencies, and a named
owner. Include pre-production, content throughput, integration, QA, platform,
live operations, and rework; a feature estimate without these is a placeholder.
For a team gap, compare required capabilities with current capacity, identify
the next bottleneck, and choose hire, contractor, tooling, training, scope cut,
or sequencing change. First steps should establish a playable proof and a
decision cadence before production scale.
